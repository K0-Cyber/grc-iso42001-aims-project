# ISO 42001 to ISO 27001 Mapping

## Purpose

This document maps ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI Management System requirements to related ISO/IEC 27001 information security management requirements.

The purpose is to show how AI governance can integrate with an existing information security management system.

## Framework Relationship

ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 27001 both follow the Annex SL management system structure.

ISO 27001 focuses on information security risks relating to confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

ISO 42001 focuses on AI governance, AI risk management, AI impact assessment, transparency, human oversight, and responsible AI lifecycle controls.

Together, they provide a stronger foundation for governing secure and responsible AI systems.

## High-Level Clause Mapping

| ISO 42001 Clause | ISO 27001 Equivalent | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Clause 4 - Context | Clause 4 - Context | Both require the organisation to understand internal and external issues, interested parties, and management system scope |
| Clause 5 - Leadership | Clause 5 - Leadership | Both require leadership commitment, policy approval, roles, responsibilities, and accountability |
| Clause 6 - Planning | Clause 6 - Planning | Both require risk assessment, risk treatment, objectives, and planning |
| Clause 6.1.4 - AI Impact Assessment | No direct equivalent | ISO 42001 adds outward-facing impact assessment for people, groups, and society |
| Clause 7 - Support | Clause 7 - Support | Both require competence, awareness, communication, and documented information |
| Clause 8 - Operation | Clause 8 - Operation | Both require operational planning and control |
| Annex A - AI Controls | Annex A - Security Controls | ISO 42001 Annex A controls focus on AI governance and lifecycle controls, while ISO 27001 Annex A controls focus on information security |
| Clause 9 - Performance Evaluation | Clause 9 - Performance Evaluation | Both require monitoring, internal audit, and management review |
| Clause 10 - Improvement | Clause 10 - Improvement | Both require nonconformity management, corrective action, and continual improvement |

## Control Area Mapping

| ISO 42001 Area | ISO 27001 Area | Connection |
|---|---|---|
| AI Policy | Information Security Policy | AI Policy can sit alongside or integrate with the information security policy framework |
| AI Roles and Responsibilities | Information Security Roles and Responsibilities | AI accountability should align with existing security governance roles |
| AI Risk Register | Information Security Risk Assessment | AI-specific risks should feed into the wider enterprise risk register |
| AI Impact Assessment | No direct equivalent | ISO 42001 adds consideration of impacts on individuals, groups, and society |
| AI Competence Framework | Competence and Awareness | AI literacy and role-specific AI training extend security awareness training |
| AI Communication Plan | Communication Requirements | AI communication supports internal and external stakeholder requirements |
| Annex A Statement of Applicability | ISO 27001 Statement of Applicability | Both document applicable controls, evidence, and implementation status |
| AI System Logging | Logging and Monitoring | AI logs support auditability, monitoring, incident response, and investigation |
| Human Oversight | Operational Control and Incident Management | Human escalation supports safe AI operation and incident handling |
| AI Supplier Governance | Supplier Relationship Security | Third-party AI providers must be assessed and contractually managed |
| AI Incident Register | Information Security Incident Management | AI incidents should integrate with security incident management |
| Continual Improvement Plan | Continual Improvement | Lessons from AI incidents, audits, and reviews drive improvements |

## Key Differences

| Area | ISO 27001 | ISO 42001 |
|---|---|---|
| Main Focus | Information security | AI management and responsible AI |
| Risk Direction | Primarily protects the organisation and information assets | Considers risks to the organisation and impacts on people, users, and society |
| Impact Assessment | No direct AI impact assessment requirement | Requires AI impact assessment |
| AI Lifecycle | Not AI-specific | Covers AI system lifecycle and AI-specific controls |
| Transparency | Not specific to AI users | Requires AI transparency and information for interested parties |
| Human Oversight | Covered indirectly through controls | Explicitly relevant to AI use and deployment |
| AI Bias and Fairness | Not directly addressed | Addressed through AI risk and impact assessment |

## Summary

ISO 42001 builds on the familiar management system structure used by ISO 27001 but adds AI-specific governance, risk, impact, transparency, accountability, and lifecycle controls.

For EugTech Solutions Ltd, ISO 27001 provides the information security foundation, while ISO 42001 provides the AI governance and responsible AI management layer.
