# Context Analysis

## Purpose

This document identifies internal and external issues that affect EugTech Solutions Ltd's Artificial Intelligence Management System.

This supports ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Clause 4.1, which requires the organisation to understand its context before designing and operating the AIMS.

## Organisation Context

EugTech Solutions Ltd is a simulated 50-person UK technology consultancy that uses AI in client service delivery and internal business operations.

The organisation uses AI to support customer service, project estimation, code review, and resource planning.

## Internal Factors

| Internal Factor | Description | AIMS Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Organisation size | EugTech is a 50-person consultancy | AIMS must be practical and proportionate |
| Growing AI use | AI is increasingly used in client services and internal operations | Requires formal AI governance |
| EugAI chatbot | Customer service chatbot deployed to 20 client organisations | Requires monitoring, transparency, and risk controls |
| Internal ML tools | AI-assisted project estimation, code review, and resource planning | Requires risk assessment and staff guidance |
| Varied AI literacy | Staff AI knowledge differs across departments | Requires AI competence framework and training |
| No dedicated AI team | AI is managed by IT, Product, and Engineering leads | Requires clear roles and RACI |
| AI growth strategy | EugTech plans to expand AI use over the next three years | Requires scalable AIMS governance |
| Existing GRC maturity | Previous ISO 27001-style governance supports security controls | Supports integration with AIMS |

## External Factors

| External Factor | Description | AIMS Impact |
|---|---|---|
| EU AI Act | AI regulation creates transparency and risk management expectations | Requires AI classification and compliance awareness |
| UK GDPR | Personal data may appear in AI prompts, logs, or support queries | Requires data protection controls |
| ICO AI guidance | UK guidance affects responsible AI and data protection expectations | Supports privacy and governance requirements |
| Client expectations | Clients increasingly request evidence of responsible AI governance | AIMS provides assurance evidence |
| Competitive pressure | AI adoption is needed to remain competitive | Requires controlled and responsible adoption |
| Rapid AI technology change | AI models and risks change quickly | Requires monitoring and continual improvement |
| Third-party AI suppliers | EugTech depends on LLM API providers | Requires supplier governance and contractual controls |
| Sector exposure | Clients include financial services, healthcare, and retail organisations | Requires stronger control over fairness, privacy, and reliability |

## Strategic AI Considerations

| Area | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Business value | AI can improve service delivery, availability, and operational efficiency |
| Risk exposure | AI can create risks relating to hallucination, bias, privacy, security, and overreliance |
| Governance need | Formal AI governance is needed to maintain trust and meet client expectations |
| Compliance need | AI use must align with emerging AI regulation and existing privacy obligations |
| Assurance need | Clients and leadership need evidence that AI is being managed responsibly |

## AI Governance Drivers

The main drivers for EugTech's AIMS are:

- Responsible AI use
- Client trust
- Regulatory readiness
- AI security
- AI transparency
- AI risk management
- AI impact assessment
- Staff AI competence
- Supplier governance
- Continual improvement

## Risks from Context

| Context Issue | Potential Risk |
|---|---|
| Rapid AI adoption | AI systems may be deployed without sufficient assessment |
| Low AI literacy | Staff may misuse AI or overtrust AI outputs |
| Third-party AI dependency | Supplier changes may affect reliability, security, or compliance |
| Client-facing AI use | Poor AI output may harm trust or contractual relationships |
| Regulatory change | New legal obligations may require updated controls |
| AI in sensitive sectors | Healthcare or financial sector clients may create heightened risk |

## Opportunities from Context

| Opportunity | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Formal AIMS implementation | Demonstrates responsible AI governance |
| Integration with ISO 27001 | Reuses security governance and controls |
| AI risk assessment | Improves decision-making before deployment |
| AI impact assessment | Identifies effects on users and society |
| Staff training | Improves safe and effective AI use |
| Cross-framework mapping | Supports ISO 27001, NIST AI RMF, and EU AI Act readiness |

## Summary

EugTech's internal and external context shows a clear need for a structured AI Management System.

The organisation is adopting AI for business value, but must manage risks relating to security, privacy, transparency, fairness, supplier dependency, regulatory expectations, and user impact.
